LEAs submit lawful information-disclosure requests through the SAHYOG Portal
(live since October 2024 under Section 79(3)(b), IT Act 2000; disclosure
requests handled via Section 94, BNSS) to VDA Service Providers — crypto
exchanges, custodial wallet providers, trading platforms. As of March 2025,
49 VDA SPs are registered with FIU-IND as reporting entities (45 domestic,
4 offshore: source — CAalley / FIU-IND Annual Report FY 2024-25). Bitget
onboarded SAHYOG in June 2025, Binance and Coinbase discussions ongoing.

The specific gap the PS names: when the suspect wallet is unhosted (non-
custodial), or the linked VASP is unknown, because funds pass through
multiple intermediary wallets before hitting a centralized exchange. Manually
tracing that path requires specialist blockchain-forensics expertise India's
local cyber cells rarely have. PHANTASM closes that gap end-to-end.

FOUR STRUCTURAL GAPS IN EXISTING MANUAL / TOOL-ASSISTED WORKFLOWS

  1. Graph explosion — naive BFS/Dijkstra traversal hits O(b^d) combinatorial
     failure at high-degree nodes (exchange hot wallets, DEX pools) where
     thousands of unrelated users share one node.

  2. Deliberate obfuscation — Sybil change-address generation, multi-hop
     peel chains, micro-structuring ("smurfing"), and mixer/bridge hops are
     specifically designed to defeat flat heuristics.

  3. Legal disconnect — commercial tools (Chainalysis Reactor, TRM Labs)
     output a visual graph and stop. They produce no automated, legally-
     compliant evidentiary trail under Section 63 BSA (Bharatiya Sakshya
     Adhiniyam 2023) or Section 65B, Indian Evidence Act.

  4. Chain-model blindness — Bitcoin and Tron use a UTXO model; Ethereum,
     BNB, Polygon, Solana use an account-balance model. Applying one
     traversal heuristic uniformly across both families weakens attribution
     quality on at least one family.

2.  PROPOSED SOLUTION — CORE CONCEPT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PHANTASM is an automated deep-learning graph engine, integrated with SAHYOG
via REST APIs, that:

  1. Ingests a suspect wallet address reported via SAHYOG or NCRP.
  2. Routes it through a chain-model adapter (UTXO vs. account-based).
  3. Applies continuous temporal-flow pruning (Hawkes process) to discard
     mixing noise, dust, and bot-driven micro-transfers.
  4. Classifies every unlabeled intermediary entity (mule wallet, staking
     pool, mixer contract, VASP deposit proxy, DEX router) BEFORE path
     search begins — classify-first, then search.
  5. Pinpoints the nearest-hop path to a FIU-IND-registered VASP or known
     global custodial exchange — i.e. the first custodial touchpoint,
     deliberately not attempting full wallet-ownership clustering (a harder,
     slower, out-of-scope problem for v1).
  6. Confidence-scores the path via Monte Carlo Random Walks.
  7. Seals the evidence trail with per-transaction Merkle proofs.
  8. Outputs a court-admissible Section 63 BSA / 65B dossier and auto-
     routes the filled disclosure/freeze request to the VASP's SAHYOG nodal
     officer.

HOW IT DIRECTLY ADDRESSES THE PS:
  Removes the manual, expertise-heavy step of tracing multi-hop transactions
  — the exact bottleneck named in SIH26182. Turns a multi-day investigation
  into a near-real-time, largely automated pipeline that ends with a pre-
  filled SAHYOG disclosure request, closing the loop from unknown wallet to
  actionable legal instrument inside one tool.

COMPONENT DEEP-DIVE — WHY EACH COMPONENT EARNS ITS PLACE

── HGT (Heterogeneous Graph Transformer) ────────────────────────────────────
Role: heterogeneous node classification.
Why this over a standard GNN:
  Blockchain transaction graphs have distinct node types (EOA, Smart
  Contract, Exchange Deposit Address) and distinct relation types (Native
  Transfer, ERC-20 Transfer, Contract Internal Call). A homogeneous GNN
  collapses all of these into one node/edge type, losing relational
  semantics. HGT uses type-dependent attention weights — separate key/query/
  value projection matrices per (src_type, edge_type, dst_type) triple —
  to classify unknown intermediate wallets without flattening them into a
  lossy space.
Research grounding:
  Hu et al., "Heterogeneous Graph Transformer," WWW 2020. Applied to crypto
  fraud detection: "Fraud Detection in Cryptocurrency Networks — An
  Exploration Using Anomaly Detection and Heterogeneous Graph Transformers,"
  Future Internet 2025, 17(1), 44 (Jan 19 2025, MDPI). Also validated in
  "On the Use of Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks for Detecting Malicious
  Activities: a Case Study with Cryptocurrencies," ACM DL 2024.

── R-GCN (Relational Graph Convolutional Network) ───────────────────────────
Role: multi-relational edge aggregation.
Why: preserves directional relation semantics across complex transaction
  paths. Distinguishes ETH native flow through a smart contract (likely a
  router or bridge) versus a direct wallet-to-wallet transfer — critical for
  not misclassifying a DEX liquidity pool as a mule wallet.

── TPP with Neural Hawkes Process ───────────────────────────────────────────
Role: continuous-time sequence modeling for obfuscation detection.
Why: laundering flows follow self-exciting temporal bursts — a cluster of
  rapid micro-transactions is statistically unlike natural human spending.
  The Hawkes intensity function λ(t) = μ + Σ α·e^(−β(t−tᵢ)) models the
  conditional rate of the next transaction given the history, flagging
  automated peel-chains and separating bot-driven structuring from organic
  timing. Standard time-discretized models (hourly/daily buckets) miss
  sub-minute burst patterns entirely.

── TGN (Temporal Graph Network) ─────────────────────────────────────────────
Role: dynamic node-state memory on streaming transactions.
Why: wallets evolve. A wallet with 2 transactions today and 500 tomorrow
  should update its embedding on new evidence, not stay frozen at training
  time. TGN maintains per-node memory vectors updated on every new block,
  enabling zero-day mule detection — i.e. catching wallets that have never
  appeared in training data but show suspicious structural behavior in
  real-time.

── PPR + Bidirectional Dijkstra with Exponential Decay ──────────────────────
Role: subgraph path extraction without combinatorial explosion.
Why: traditional BFS breaks catastrophically at high-degree nodes — a major
  exchange hot wallet may have 500,000+ unique transaction partners. PPR
  initialized at the suspect seed wallet, combined with edge-weight decay
  (larger/older transfers get lower weight), prioritizes the fastest, high-
  value-transfer route directly to the nearest custodial VASP. This is the
  mechanical implementation of "classify-then-search": once a node is HGT/
  R-GCN-tagged as a terminal VASP node, PPR treats it as an absorption state
  and stops expanding past it, rather than continuing to branch into that
  node's thousands of other connections.

── Monte Carlo Random Walks with Restart (RWR) ──────────────────────────────
Role: confidence estimation.
Why: simulates thousands of probabilistic traversal paths across uncertain
  hops. Outputs a deterministic Attribution Confidence Score (0–100%) with
  a credible interval rather than a binary verdict. Below a configurable
  threshold, cases route to mandatory human review — confidence scoring
  prevents auto-action on low-certainty attribution.

── Blockchain-Native Additions ──────────────────────────────────────────────

  (a) CHAIN-MODEL-AWARE HEURISTICS (UTXO vs. Account-based)
      Bitcoin and Tron use UTXO: each transaction consumes one or more
      unspent outputs and creates new ones. Co-spend clustering — if N
      inputs are consumed in one transaction, they are very likely owned by
      the same controller — is the dominant entity-attribution heuristic
      on UTXO chains. Ethereum/BNB/Polygon use account balances: nonce-
      sequencing (each sender increments a counter), fan-in/fan-out pattern
      analysis, and contract-call tracing are the relevant heuristics there.
      Applying one generic logic uniformly degrades attribution quality on
      at least one chain family. PHANTASM's chain-adapter layer applies the
      correct heuristic per chain.

  (b) CHAIN-SHARDED GRAPH STORAGE
      Graph DB partitioned by blockchain (Bitcoin shard, Ethereum shard,
      Tron shard, etc.), each an independent Neo4j/Memgraph partition.
      Consistent-hashing routing sends a wallet address to its correct
      shard based on chain type. Adding a 7th blockchain = adding a shard,
      not rebalancing the whole system. A thin cross-chain "bridge index"
      stitches shard boundaries at known bridge/swap contract addresses
      when a trace crosses chains. This is what makes the "cross-chain +
      DeFi-bridge tracing" claim actually scale rather than choke on one
      monolithic graph as data volume reaches national case load.

  (c) MERKLE-PROOF-SEALED EVIDENCE TRAIL
      Every block header commits to its transactions via a Merkle root.
      The final dossier includes the Merkle proof for each transaction in
      the traced path — a small, deterministic cryptographic structure
      (O(log N) hashes) letting any third party (court, defense counsel,
      auditor) independently verify that the cited transaction is genuinely
      included in the cited block, without trusting PHANTASM's own claim.
      This is what turns "court-admissible" from a marketing phrase into a
      verifiable mechanism directly supporting Section 65B / Section 63
      BSA evidentiary requirements.

  (d) CONFIRMATION-DEPTH FILTERING
      Blockchains occasionally reorganize (an orphaned block gets replaced).
      PHANTASM waits for a chain-specific minimum confirmation depth before
      treating a transaction as final and including it in the attribution
      path. Avoids building a case on data that could later be reorganized
      away. Bitcoin: 6 confirmations standard. Ethereum: ≥12 post-Merge.
      Tron: configurable. This is a small but correct engineering detail
      that most commercial tools silently skip.

HONEST FEASIBILITY CAVEAT:
  This is a prototype-scale timeline. Attribution accuracy, confirmation-
  depth thresholds per chain, and cross-chain bridge coverage should be
  presented as measured (e.g. precision/recall on the Elliptic labeled
  subset, F1-score on held-out test set) rather than assumed at production-
  grade numbers. Classifier accuracy on zero-day wallets with sparse
  transaction history will be genuinely lower than on established wallets —
  acknowledge this, don't hide it.

10. STRATEGIC DEFENSE — ANTICIPATED JUDGE QUESTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Q: "Why not just use Chainalysis or TRM Labs?"

A: Both are strong entity-clustering platforms. But they are:
   (a) Closed-source, expensive for local cyber cells — per-seat licensing
       scales into tens of thousands of USD, unaffordable for district-level
       cyber police in India.
   (b) Primarily static, pre-tagged address database lookups — they do not
       dynamically classify previously unseen mule wallets via structural/
       temporal graph modeling.
   (c) Completely disconnected from SAHYOG — there is no native integration
       for India's disclosure-request workflow.
   PHANTASM targets one narrow, fast query (nearest custodial hop) with
   dynamic HGT/TGN/Hawkes models to catch unseen mule wallets, and plugs
   directly into SAHYOG at a fraction of the licensing cost. It is a
   complement to, not a replacement for, those platforms in contexts where
   they are available.


Q: "How do you handle mixing services like Tornado Cash or cross-chain
   bridges like Stargate?"

A: Two-pronged:
   (1) Mixer deposit smart contracts are identified via R-GCN node
       classification at the contract-bytecode signature level. The engine
       does not traverse internal pool state blindly — it flags the mixer
       node, isolates deposit timestamp and denomination, and runs temporal
       correlation on withdrawal events across candidate VASPs within that
       time window.
   (2) Cross-chain bridges are tracked via the bridge-index layer in the
       sharded graph DB — bridge contract addresses on both source and
       destination chains are pre-labeled, so a cross-chain hop is treated
       as a typed edge in the multigraph, not a dead end. FATF Travel Rule
       data-sharing supplements where inter-VASP data is available.
   This does NOT guarantee full trace through a well-operated mixer — we
   disclose this limitation honestly and present a confidence score
   reflecting the uncertainty.


Q: "What if the attribution is wrong?"

A: Every output carries a Monte Carlo–derived confidence score AND a full
   Merkle-proof-backed evidence trail. There is no binary verdict. Below a
   configurable confidence threshold, the case routes to mandatory human
   review — a trained LEA analyst makes the final call. The Merkle proofs
   allow any party (defense counsel, judge, independent auditor) to verify
   every cited transaction independently. Wrong attribution is a known risk
   we engineer around, not one we paper over.


Q: "How does this scale beyond a hackathon demo to national case volume?"

A: The chain-sharded graph DB and per-chain Celery worker pools are
   specifically designed for horizontal scaling: adding a 7th blockchain
   means adding a DB shard and a worker pool, not re-architecting the core.
   Redis handles live edge caching so repeated wallet lookups (the same
   exchange hot wallet queried in 1,000 separate cases) are served from
   memory rather than re-queried from chain APIs. National scale means
   provisioning more shards and workers on existing government cloud
   infrastructure, not a new system design.


Q: "Is the Elliptic dataset enough to train a production-grade classifier?"

A: No — and we say so. Elliptic is the best publicly available labeled
   Bitcoin AML benchmark and is the correct starting point for a prototype
   and for benchmarking against published baselines (MDST-GNN, TGN, etc.).
   Production deployment would require FIU-IND-provided labeled case data
   from Indian LEA investigations to fine-tune the model on India-specific
   laundering typologies. That is out of scope for SIH but is the correct
   next step for a production system, and we design the fine-tuning pipeline
   into the architecture from day one.


Q: "What is the actual latency per wallet trace?"

A: We will benchmark this on the prototype and report the measured figure.
   We do not assert a latency claim not yet measured. Factors that dominate
   latency: API rate limits on public block-explorer endpoints (mitigated
   by caching and multi-source ingestion), graph DB query time on the
   classified subgraph, and Monte Carlo simulation sample count. We will
   present the latency-accuracy trade-off curve as part of the demo.
